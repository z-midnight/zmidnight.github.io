After Midnight

There are things I only remember when the world becomes quiet. Names I no longer say. Places I no longer go. People I no longer wait for. Perhaps healing is not forgetting. Perhaps it is simply learning how to remember without reaching back.

Things I Never Said

I kept the words behind my teeth, afraid that saying them would make them real. So I carried them through the nights, until silence became heavier than the truth.

Somewhere Between

The Poems I Buried

I deleted every poem I wrote for you, every tender line I once thought true. Not because the words were never bright, but because they kept me waiting every night. I grew tired of the girl between the lines, who held on to ghosts and called them signs. So I buried your name where old verses sleep, and gave the words to silence, dark and deep. Perhaps forgetting isn't losing what we knew, but learning how to write without writing you. No more leaving empty spaces for you to stay— some stories must be mourned, then fade away.

I Hold It In

Sometimes I want to tell you the things I hide inside, the words I long to tell you, the tears I cannot hide. I want to call your name, to tell you what I feel, but silence is my shame, so I pretend to heal. Because you’re not mine to keep, and I know where you belong, so I bury what runs deep, and swallow every song. I miss you when it’s night, when the world has gone to sleep, I love you out of sight, where no one sees how deep. So I’ll hold back every plea, and let my feelings fade, you’ll never know what you mean to me— or how much love I’ve betrayed.

The Cartography of You

I loved you in absentia, where longing wears no name, a clandestine constellation that burns without a flame. You were my ineffable ache, a syllable I couldn’t say, an ephemeral kind of forever I watched from miles away. I kept you in the liminal spaces— between farewell and desire, where proximity is a fiction and silence speaks entire. Perhaps I was merely quixotic, mistaking distance for fate, yet every road my heart remembered still led me to your gate. So I loved you vicariously, through moments never mine, a melancholic devotion drawn beyond the edge of time. And if love is an enigma, then let me remain unsure— for some souls are meant to be cherished, but never meant to be yours.

An Ontology of Distance

I loved you beyond the epistemology of proximity, where presence is merely a conjecture and absence, an immutable theorem. You existed somewhere between phenomenology and impossibility— a recurring apparition my consciousness refused to classify as illusion. I made an apocryphal cathedral of your existence, worshipping the silhouette of a possibility whose corporeality was never mine to possess. How perverse, this affection— to be simultaneously intimate and infinitely estranged, to know the geography of your soul without ever being granted its coordinates. Perhaps I was ontologically delusional, mistaking coincidence for providence, silence for reciprocity, and the gravitational pull of longing for some metaphysical indication that you, too, remembered me. Yet I remained— an anachronistic heart in a chronology that had no room for us, loving you in the margins of a narrative that never named me. And if someday you ask what became of all that affection— nothing. It simply transmuted. Into distance. Into silence. Into the quiet atavism of a feeling that refuses to die simply because it was never allowed to live.

An Ontology of Distance (soft version)

I loved you past the reach of space, where absence wore a familiar face. You were never truly mine to hold, yet losing you still left me cold. You lived somewhere between dream and truth, a fading echo of my youth. I called it fate, I called it sign, when all along, you were never mine. I built a cathedral out of “maybe,” made every silence feel less empty. I prayed to shadows, soft and thin, for just one sign you felt it within. How cruel is love that cannot stay, that blooms in night and dies by day; to know your soul, yet miss your hand, to love someone you cannot stand beside, nor understand. Perhaps I was foolish, lost in the glow, mistaking the longing for something I’d know. I turned every silence into a reply, every coincidence into “you and I.” But you never promised, never confessed, never gave my restless heart a place to rest. And that was the wound I could never explain— you never broke me, yet I carried the pain. So I stayed in the margins, quiet and small, loving a shadow I could never call. I wrote you in verses, I kept you in rhyme, while you remained somewhere outside my time. And if someday you ask where the love went— It stayed. It became every night I remember your name, every quiet hour that feels just the same. It became distance, it became goodbye, a love with no ending, because it never got its start in the first place— and that is why. I did not lose you beneath some distant sky. I never had you. And still— I have to learn how to say goodbye.
