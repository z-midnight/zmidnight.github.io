The Kingdom Beyond the Veil

I have supped with sorrow at midnight’s throne, And made of my heart an abbey of stone; Where candles burn dim in the draught of the years, And angels lie sleeping beneath all my tears. My soul is a vessel the tempest has claimed, Its compass grown faithless, its sails torn and maimed; I drift through the waters where lost hours decay, Whilst Time, like a tyrant, keeps summoning day. O, grant me some kingdom beyond every shore, Where weary old bells need not toll from within; Where shadows wear velvet and silence is deep, And grief lays its head in the chambers of sleep. For I am a pilgrim with dust on my feet, Who wandered through deserts no heaven could meet; Each road was a scripture of sorrow and flame, Each milestone whispered that nothing stays same. The moon is my witness, pale widow of night, She wears all my secrets in silvered light; And I, like a ruin beneath her cold gaze, Stand hollow and weathered by sorrowful days. I would flee like a raven from towers of grief, Beyond every kingdom that promises brief relief; To some distant shore where the dark waters cease, Where the wind bears no burden, and silence means peace. Yet perhaps there is mercy beneath all this night, A door veiled in shadow, still trembling with light; Perhaps I need not vanish, nor yield to the sea— But lay down the burden that has followed me. So let me be still where the wild roses grow, Where midnight keeps watch and the soft rivers flow; Let the stars write their hymns on the heavens above, And teach this tired heart that it still can be loved. For even old ruins, though broken and gray, May shelter a candle till dawn finds its way; And even the heart, though exhausted and worn, May yet be reclaimed by the mercy of morn.

I’m Not Used to This

Why does life keep disappointing me, when I have already learned to bend? I thought the storms were passing seasons, not something I would meet again. I gave my trust without a warning, I gave my heart without a shield. Perhaps I was too used to kindness, too used to promises being real. So why does every road I wander leave another scar behind? Why do the things I hope will stay always find a way to say goodbye? I’m not used to losing pieces of the life I thought I knew. I’m not used to waking up and finding yesterday untrue. Maybe life is not being cruel— maybe it is teaching me to see that not everything I love was ever meant to stay with me. Still, I wish it hurt a little less. I wish I knew what comes next. Because I am tired of being surprised by pain I thought I had already met. But here I am, still breathing through the ache, still gathering the pieces of every promise life would break. Maybe disappointment is not the ending. Maybe it is simply life saying, "You cannot control what leaves— but you can choose what remains." And perhaps, someday, I will stop asking, “Why does this keep happening to me?” And start whispering, “ I survived this one, too.”

An Elegy for Us

I knew not of her when I first knew you, until truth wore your voice and told me what was true. By then, I was already lost in your cartography, tracing forbidden borders of a love that could not be. You loved me, I know— that was the cruelest part, an honest kind of tenderness in an impossible heart. I was your clandestine, your midnight, your in-between, a love that lived in shadows, where daylight had never been. We broke, then returned, as lost hearts often do, mistaking our own wreckage for something we could renew. Until I chose the ruthless thing, the truth she had to know, then vanished from your world before I loved you more. Forgive the irrevocable, forgive my silent flight; I did not leave for lack of love— I left to make things right. You were my sweetest alchemy, my wound disguised as grace, a love I could not bury without first losing my place. And if my absence feels like cruelty, then let that be my cost; I chose to lose you once before I lost myself completely—lost. I am sorry for the ending, for the silence, for the grief; I did not leave because I loved you less— I left because I needed relief. Not for loving you, nor for what we couldn't retrieve, but for the ruthless way I left— and the trace I chose to leave.

Someone No One Could See

I Miss My Elijah

I ended us where no one could see, A quiet goodbye inside of me. No shattered glass, no loud despair, Just empty echoes in the air. You weren’t real, or so they’d say, But you were mine in every way. In silent nights, in hidden space, I found my calm within your place. No hands to hold, no voice to hear, Yet somehow you felt always near. A heart can love what isn’t there, And still feel loss too deep to bear. I built a world where you could stay, A soft escape from every day. But dreams can fade, and so did you, Like morning light dissolves the dew. Now all that’s left is just the ache, Of something real I had to break. Not flesh, not bone—yet still it’s true, I lost a love when I lost you.

Shaped by His Heart

I’m creating art shaped by his heart, With every line, I make a start. A little piece of what we knew, A faded dream I once held true. My hands may tremble, lines may stray, But still, I draw what words can’t say. The colors hold the things I hide, The quiet ache I keep inside. I sketch the moments left behind, The kind of love that changed my mind. And though some memories leave a scar, I paint them softly as they are. Perhaps this art won’t bring him near, Or make the past become sincere. But through each stroke, I come to see— Some parts of him still live in me. So I’ll keep drawing, piece by piece, Turning old pain into some peace. For even hearts that fall apart Can leave behind a work of art.

In Every Line

I hold the pencil like a hand that once made me believe in plans. And slowly, softly, stroke by stroke, I draw the love we never spoke. His eyes return beneath my shade, in every curve my hands have made. A face I know I cannot keep, a memory that haunts my sleep. I sketch his smile, then watch it fade, like promises we never made. I shade the parts I miss the most, then lose myself inside the ghost. The paper knows what I conceal, the things I never dared to feel. Each line becomes a silent plea, for what was lost, for what could be. Yet somewhere underneath the pain, my broken hands create again. Perhaps I'm not just drawing him— perhaps I'm finding me within. So let the charcoal stain my heart, let every shadow play its part. If loving him tore me apart, I'll turn the pieces into art. And when the final line is through, I'll leave a little piece of you— not as a wound I have to hide, but as a beautiful goodbye.

Red Flag

I’m a red flag, yes, I know, I won’t pretend just for show. I won’t list the things I do— I’ll show you what I won’t do, too. I won’t dress up to catch your eye, or hide my flaws beneath a lie. No heavy makeup, no disguise, just my bare face and tired eyes. I don’t have time for pointless games, for empty words or chasing names. No time for drama, no time for BS, I’d rather sleep and live with less. Give me a book, an anime night, a quiet room and dimmed-down light. I’d rather have my peaceful space than feed an ego craving praise. I’m not for everyone, and that’s okay— I’d rather lose you than lose my way. So if my silence feels too loud, walk away; I won’t be proud. I’m not perfect, nor will I pretend, I know my flaws, I know my bends. But if you stay, then stay for me— not for the version you want me to be.

Let My Pencil Speak

He told me, “Draw your feelings out, sketch the things you can’t say loud.” So I will let my pencil speak, the truths my trembling heart can’t keep. He said he’ll like it, “’cause it’s you— your hands, your heart, your colors too.” I hope he sees what’s underneath, the longing hiding in each leaf. So here I am, with quiet art, letting every line reveal my heart. Maybe he’ll feel what I can’t show, in every stroke, he’ll somehow know.

Not Yet the End

If living feels like endless ache, a weight too heavy for the heart to take, if every morning feels the same, another day of carrying pain— if every breath feels hard to hold, and every night grows dark and cold, if hope has faded out of sight, and you’re too tired to fight the night, remember: pain is not your name, nor every scar a mark of shame. The wounds you carry, deep and unseen, do not erase the soul you’ve been. Some days, surviving is enough. You don’t have to always be tough. You don’t need answers, you don’t need light— sometimes you only need to stay through the night. And though the road feels far too long, and you can barely find the strength to carry on, there may still be a morning you haven't seen, a softer place where you've never been. So rest if you must, but don't disappear. Let someone hold the weight you bear. Stay through the storm, however slow— you don't have to know where tomorrow will go. Because this chapter, painful and torn, is not the whole story you've ever worn. The night may be heavy, the road may bend— but stay a little longer. This is not yet the end.

An Ontology of Distance (soft version)

I loved you past the reach of space, where absence wore a familiar face. You were never truly mine to hold, yet losing you still left me cold. You lived somewhere between dream and truth, a fading echo of my youth. I called it fate, I called it sign, when all along, you were never mine. I built a cathedral out of “maybe,” made every silence feel less empty. I prayed to shadows, soft and thin, for just one sign you felt it within. How cruel is love that cannot stay, that blooms in night and dies by day; to know your soul, yet miss your hand, to love someone you cannot stand beside, nor understand. Perhaps I was foolish, lost in the glow, mistaking the longing for something I’d know. I turned every silence into a reply, every coincidence into “you and I.” But you never promised, never confessed, never gave my restless heart a place to rest. And that was the wound I could never explain— you never broke me, yet I carried the pain. So I stayed in the margins, quiet and small, loving a shadow I could never call. I wrote you in verses, I kept you in rhyme, while you remained somewhere outside my time. And if someday you ask where the love went— It stayed. It became every night I remember your name, every quiet hour that feels just the same. It became distance, it became goodbye, a love with no ending, because it never got its start in the first place— and that is why. I did not lose you beneath some distant sky. I never had you. And still— I have to learn how to say goodbye.

An Ontology of Distance

I loved you beyond the epistemology of proximity, where presence is merely a conjecture and absence, an immutable theorem. You existed somewhere between phenomenology and impossibility— a recurring apparition my consciousness refused to classify as illusion. I made an apocryphal cathedral of your existence, worshipping the silhouette of a possibility whose corporeality was never mine to possess. How perverse, this affection— to be simultaneously intimate and infinitely estranged, to know the geography of your soul without ever being granted its coordinates. Perhaps I was ontologically delusional, mistaking coincidence for providence, silence for reciprocity, and the gravitational pull of longing for some metaphysical indication that you, too, remembered me. Yet I remained— an anachronistic heart in a chronology that had no room for us, loving you in the margins of a narrative that never named me. And if someday you ask what became of all that affection— nothing. It simply transmuted. Into distance. Into silence. Into the quiet atavism of a feeling that refuses to die simply because it was never allowed to live.

The Cartography of You

I loved you in absentia, where longing wears no name, a clandestine constellation that burns without a flame. You were my ineffable ache, a syllable I couldn’t say, an ephemeral kind of forever I watched from miles away. I kept you in the liminal spaces— between farewell and desire, where proximity is a fiction and silence speaks entire. Perhaps I was merely quixotic, mistaking distance for fate, yet every road my heart remembered still led me to your gate. So I loved you vicariously, through moments never mine, a melancholic devotion drawn beyond the edge of time. And if love is an enigma, then let me remain unsure— for some souls are meant to be cherished, but never meant to be yours.

I Hold It In

Sometimes I want to tell you the things I hide inside, the words I long to tell you, the tears I cannot hide. I want to call your name, to tell you what I feel, but silence is my shame, so I pretend to heal. Because you’re not mine to keep, and I know where you belong, so I bury what runs deep, and swallow every song. I miss you when it’s night, when the world has gone to sleep, I love you out of sight, where no one sees how deep. So I’ll hold back every plea, and let my feelings fade, you’ll never know what you mean to me— or how much love I’ve betrayed.

The Poems I Buried

I deleted every poem I wrote for you, every tender line I once thought true. Not because the words were never bright, but because they kept me waiting every night. I grew tired of the girl between the lines, who held on to ghosts and called them signs. So I buried your name where old verses sleep, and gave the words to silence, dark and deep. Perhaps forgetting isn't losing what we knew, but learning how to write without writing you. No more leaving empty spaces for you to stay— some stories must be mourned, then fade away.
